Every few months someone asks why our sourdough tastes different from the loaf they bake at home. The honest answer is usually **the flour**, not the starter.

We test a lot of flour. Most of it is fine. A few bags make bread that is *noticeably* better, and those are the ones we buy again.

## What we look for

Three numbers on a spec sheet tell us most of what we need:

- **Protein:** 11.5 to 12.5 percent gives a strong but extensible dough.
- **Ash:** a little higher ash means more of the bran stays in, and more flavour.
- **Milling date:** flour older than three months loses aroma fast.

<Callout type="tip">
Ask your mill for the **milling date**, not the best-before date. They are often a year apart.
</Callout>

## The three flours on our shelf

| Flour | Protein | What we use it for |
| ----- | ------- | ------------------ |
| T65 bread flour | 12% | Most of every loaf |
| Whole rye | 9% | Flavour and a lively starter |
| T80 high-extraction | 11.5% | Our country loaf |

Rye does most of the work in our starter. A spoonful in every feed keeps it active through the cold months.

## Try this at home

Bake two loaves on the same day with the same starter. Change *only* the flour. Taste them side by side the next morning, and you will know more than any spec sheet can tell you.
